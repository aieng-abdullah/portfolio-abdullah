About

Turning research into
real-world AI systems.

I didn't start with a roadmap. I started with curiosity — and a stubborn need to understand how things actually work under the hood.

I'm Abdullah Al Arif, an AI Engineer from Bangladesh. My journey into AI started right after finishing my high school diploma — driven by the math, the papers, and a lot of broken code. I got into this field by going deep: reading the math before touching the framework, tracing the attention mechanism before using a transformer, following the gradient all the way back before trusting a training loop.

During my internship at Software Intelligence Ltd, I stopped treating AI as magic and started treating it as infrastructure. I built production RAG pipelines, reduced hallucination through Pydantic citation enforcement and cross-encoder reranking — tracked with Ragas evaluation — and integrated MCP and A2A protocols end-to-end. That's where I learned the part most tutorials skip: evaluation, observability, and what happens when your system meets real data.

I'm still early in my career. But I build seriously, document what I learn, and ship things that work beyond the demo. I believe the engineers who will matter in this decade aren't the ones who know the most APIs — they're the ones who understand the systems deeply enough to fix them when they break.

That's what I'm working toward.

Philosophy

Hard truths about building
AI that ships.

01

Evaluation is the real skill.

Anyone can call an API. The hard part is knowing whether it works — golden datasets, hallucination detection, LLM-as-judge, and drift monitoring are what separate prototypes from systems you can trust in production.

02

Production thinking beats accuracy.

A model that scores 0.95 in a notebook but can't handle latency, cost, or scale is a liability. I optimize for the full stack: serving, observability, failure modes, and the infrastructure that keeps AI reliable under real load.

03

Ship, don't slide.

95% of AI roles are applied, not research. I focus on end-to-end delivery — from data pipeline to deployed API — because companies hire engineers who can build, deploy, scale, and measure. Not just present.
